# What does photosensitivity mean?

Photosensitivity means that skin may react more strongly to sunlight or UV light after contact with certain substances.

With essential oils, this is especially important for some citrus oils.

## The simple answer

If an oil may be photosensitive and you apply it to the skin, do not ignore sun or UV exposure afterwards.

Important basics:

- read the product label
- follow warnings
- plan topical use carefully
- avoid sun exposure if the label warns about it
- consider dilution
- ask when unsure

## Which oils can be relevant?

Some citrus oils are commonly discussed in connection with photosensitivity.

Depending on the product and production method, this may include:

- Lemon
- Lime
- Bergamot
- Grapefruit
- Wild Orange

Not every product is the same. Current product information matters.

## Aromatic use is different from topical use

Photosensitivity mainly concerns skin application.

An oil in a diffuser is different from an oil applied to sun-exposed skin.

Still, every oil should be used consciously.

## A common beginner mistake

A common mistake is:

Apply citrus oil to the skin and then go into the sun.

This is exactly why photosensitivity belongs in the first safety lessons.

## What to avoid

Avoid:

- citrus oils on skin before sun exposure
- DIY skin recipes without checking
- ignoring label warnings
- strong sun after topical application
- tanning beds or UV exposure after warned products
- generalizing one product’s guidance to all products

## In short

Photosensitivity is a safety note for certain oils when used topically before sun or UV exposure.

If you are unsure, do not apply the oil to sun-exposed skin and ask first.
