# Coriander

Coriander is a single essential oil with a clear scent profile and its own role in the doTERRA range. On Kyle Oils, this oil is not explained as a quick solution for complaints, but as part of scent, care, everyday life, product choice and safe guidance.

## Botanical name

Coriandrum sativum

## Scent family

Herbal and spice oil

## Scent profile

green, spicy, slightly sweet, herbal and fresh

## Core area

Kitchen, scent families & digestive feel

## What doTERRA officially points toward

doTERRA distinguishes Coriander from the seed and Cilantro from the leaf of the same plant. This matters for the Oil Library because both profiles feel different.

Kyle Oils translates this product context into language visitors can understand: What do I smell? Why are people looking for this oil? Where does it fit into routines? Which safety boundaries matter?

## Safe public positioning

This oil can be placed publicly like this:

- as a learning oil for the difference between coriander leaf and coriander seed
- for green-spicy scent logic in food routines
- as a bridge between herbal oil and spice oil
- for people who want to distinguish similar plant profiles more clearly

## What we do not write

These statements do not belong on the public website:

- treat digestion
- promise mood or positive attitude
- recommend internal use without label context

## How to get to know this oil

Do not start with the question of what an oil supposedly can do. Start with four simpler questions:

- How does it smell to you?
- Is it more bright, warm, herbal, woody, spicy or deep?
- Does it fit room scent, care, kitchen logic, massage or evening routine?
- Which use is intended by the label?

This builds real oil competence without relying on exaggerated promises.

## Safety context

Coriander is concentrated and should not be confused with the kitchen spice. Small amounts, label and personal context matter more than spontaneous recipes.

## Guidance from Kyle

If you want to know whether Coriander fits your situation, ask Kyle. Especially with strong, warm, skin-related or food-adjacent oils, personal context is more useful than a quick internet rule.
