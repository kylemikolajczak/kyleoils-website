# Cardamom

Cardamom is a single essential oil with a clear scent profile and its own role in the doTERRA range. On Kyle Oils, this oil is not explained as a quick solution for complaints, but as part of scent, care, everyday life, product choice and safe guidance.

## Botanical name

Elettaria cardamomum

## Scent family

Spice oil

## Scent profile

warm, spicy, soft, slightly sweet and aromatic

## Core area

Digestive feel, food routines & warm scent profiles

## What doTERRA officially points toward

doTERRA lists Cardamom as a warm spice oil. In the safe Kyle Oils context, it becomes a profile for food routines, scent and product choice.

Kyle Oils translates this product context into language visitors can understand: What do I smell? Why are people looking for this oil? Where does it fit into routines? Which safety boundaries matter?

## Safe public positioning

This oil can be placed publicly like this:

- for warm diffuser blends around kitchen and everyday life
- as a spice profile in the digestive feel and food routines area
- for people who want to understand single oils before choosing ZenGest
- as a softer spice profile compared with very strong hot oils

## What we do not write

These statements do not belong on the public website:

- relieve stomach pain
- treat digestion
- publish capsule recipes for complaints

## How to get to know this oil

Do not start with the question of what an oil supposedly can do. Start with four simpler questions:

- How does it smell to you?
- Is it more bright, warm, herbal, woody, spicy or deep?
- Does it fit room scent, care, kitchen logic, massage or evening routine?
- Which use is intended by the label?

This builds real oil competence without relying on exaggerated promises.

## Safety context

Cardamom remains a concentrated essential oil. Dilute for topical use, be careful around children and sensitive skin and place internal use only by label.

## Guidance from Kyle

If you want to know whether Cardamom fits your situation, ask Kyle. Especially with strong, warm, skin-related or food-adjacent oils, personal context is more useful than a quick internet rule.
