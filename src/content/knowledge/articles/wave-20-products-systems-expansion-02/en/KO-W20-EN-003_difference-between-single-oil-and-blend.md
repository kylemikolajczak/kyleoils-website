# Difference between single oil and blend

Single oils and blends are not better or worse. They play different roles. When you understand the difference, product choices become clearer.

## What is a single oil?

A single oil comes from one clearly named plant or plant part. It helps you understand one scent profile, one product label and one use idea at a time. Examples include Lavender, Lemon, Peppermint, Cedarwood and Bergamot.

## What is a blend?

A blend combines several essential oils in one product. This creates a finished scent profile. For beginners, this can be practical because the composition is already prepared. Still, it matters to know which oils are included.

## When single oils make sense

Single oils are helpful when you want to learn. You can better notice what you like, which scent family fits and how one oil feels in a diffuser or diluted routine.

## When blends make sense

Blends are practical when you want a clear scent idea without combining many oils yourself. They can simplify the start, but they should not be seen as a shortcut around safety.

## In short

A single oil means: understand and explore clearly. A blend means: ready-made scent profile and simple orientation. Both can make sense when they fit your routine.

## What you can do with this

Before deciding, write down three things: What do I actually want to use in everyday life? Which safety questions matter? What would be a small, useful first step?
