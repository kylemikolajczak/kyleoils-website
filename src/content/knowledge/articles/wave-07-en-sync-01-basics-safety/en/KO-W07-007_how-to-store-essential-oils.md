# How to store essential oils

Storing essential oils well is part of safe use.

Essential oils are concentrated products. They should not be left anywhere, especially if children, pets or visitors are in the home.

A simple storage system can make your routine calmer and safer.

## Keep oils away from heat and direct sunlight

Essential oils should generally be stored away from heat and direct sunlight.

Heat and light can affect product quality over time. A cool, dry place is usually better than a windowsill, car, bathroom shelf near a hot shower or sunny kitchen counter.

## Keep bottles closed

Always close the cap after use.

This helps protect the oil and reduces the chance of spills, scent evaporation or accidental contact.

A small habit makes a big difference: use it, close it, put it away.

## Keep oils out of reach of children

Essential oils should be stored where children cannot easily access them.

Child-resistant does not mean child-proof. Do not rely only on caps or packaging.

A high shelf, locked box or dedicated storage case can be helpful depending on your home.

## Store strong oils with extra care

Some oils are more intense and should be handled with extra attention.

Strong oils should not be left in bags, bathrooms, cars or places where someone might use them casually without understanding them.

If you have oils such as Oregano, Cinnamon Bark, Clove, Thyme or similar intense products, make sure they are clearly stored and not confused with gentle daily oils.

## Label your blends

If you make a diluted roll-on or blend, label it.

Include:

- what oil or oils are inside
- what carrier oil was used
- approximate dilution if known
- date made
- who it is for

This prevents confusion later.

## Do not store oils in the car

Cars can become hot or cold very quickly. This is usually not ideal for essential oil storage.

If you take oils with you, bring them back inside rather than leaving them in the car.

## Keep an overview

A simple oil box or shelf can help you see what you have.

Organize oils by:

- daily use
- diffuser oils
- strong oils
- blends
- products for later
- oils that need special safety notes

You do not need a complicated system. You need one that prevents chaos.

## Check old oils

If an oil smells unusual, has changed significantly or has been stored poorly, be cautious.

When in doubt, do not use it on the skin. Ask before using old or questionable products.

## Common storage mistakes

- leaving oils in direct sun
- leaving bottles open
- storing oils where children can reach them
- keeping oils in the car
- not labeling homemade blends
- mixing strong oils with daily gentle products
- not noticing expired or old products
- storing everything loose in a bag

## Summary

Good storage makes essential oil use safer, cleaner and easier.

Keep oils cool, closed, organized and out of reach of children. Treat strong oils with extra respect.
